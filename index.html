The gap, in plain terms

Huntingdon diners can already get a good North Indian curry. What they can't get is Kerala food: coconut, curry leaf, seafood, appams, fish moilee and beef fry. It's named a "cuisine to know for 2026" by Datassential.

Strengths and risks

Strengths

  • No direct Kerala rivals in PE29
  • Prices undercut the local entry point
  • Strong food and visual identity for marketing

Weaknesses

  • Few locals know Kerala food by name
  • Needs specialist ingredients: curry leaf, coconut, red rice
  • Nine rivals have loyal customers and many reviews

Opportunities

  • Biryani up 19% and regional Indian food rising fastest
  • 54% to 76% of UK diners seek out new cuisines
  • Affluent commuters support mid-tier pricing

Threats

  • Net 3 to 4 UK hospitality venues closing daily in early 2026
  • Mamas and Indian Palace hold review-driven loyalty
  • Delivery-only rivals compete hard on price

What to do next

  1. Lead with "Kerala", not "Indian"It's the clearest difference from nine generalist rivals.
  2. Anchor the menu with 3 to 4 signature dishesAppam and stew, fish moilee, beef fry and a Kerala-style biryani.
  3. Use starters as the trial£2.99 to £4.99 small plates (banana chips, cutlets, pepper prawns) let new diners try before committing to a main.
  4. Build a full financial modelAdd real rent, staffing and food costs for your site.
